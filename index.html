Problem Solving: Routine vs. Non-Routine Real-Life Applications

Routine Problems: Direct application of established formulas, algorithms, or standard computational procedures (e.g., finding the area of a circle given the radius).

Non-Routine Real-Life Applications: Contextualized practical tasks (such as navigating Tabaco Port, designing local market stalls, or viewing Mayon Volcano angles) where the solution path is not immediately obvious, requiring critical thinking, heuristic strategies, and spatial reasoning.

Problem-Solving Guides & Heuristics

Choose between the Standard Routine Strategy and Non-Routine Heuristic Guide:

George Polya's 4-Step Routine Strategy:

Step 1

Understand the Problem: Identify the given values, unknowns, and standard conditions.

Step 2

Devise a Plan: Select the correct standard formula or theorem that connects the given data to the target.

Step 3

Carry Out the Plan: Substitute values into the formula and execute calculations accurately.

Step 4

Look Back: Check computation steps, verify units, and ensure reasonableness.

Non-Routine Heuristic Strategies for Real-Life Applications:

Heuristic 1

Draw a Diagram / Visualize: Sketch real-life spatial relationships (e.g., Tabaco Port vectors or church rooftops).

Heuristic 2

Look for a Pattern: Examine sequence changes, tiling arrangements, or repetitive geometric structures.

Heuristic 3

Work Backwards: Start from the desired real-world goal state and trace back to initial conditions.

Heuristic 4

Solve a Simpler Sub-Problem: Break down complex architectural or practical dimensions into manageable parts.

Parts of Geometry for First-Year BSED Math (College Readiness Focus)

To transition from secondary school mathematics to tertiary educator preparation, first-year BSED Mathematics students must master specific geometric domains:


  • Axiomatic Systems & Incidence Geometry: Understanding undefined terms (points, lines, planes), postulates, and deductive logical frameworks rather than relying solely on visual intuition.
  • Euclidean & Non-Euclidean Geometry: Investigating parallel postulates, absolute geometry, and spherical or hyperbolic geometries to expand spatial reasoning.
  • Transformation Geometry: Analyzing geometric properties via rigid motions (translations, rotations, reflections, glide reflections) and similarity transformations.
  • Coordinate & Analytical Geometry: Bridging algebra and geometry through vector spaces, loci equations, conic sections, and analytic proofs of geometric theorems.
  • Rigorous Proof Techniques: Developing direct proofs, indirect proofs (contradiction/contrapositive), and geometric constructions to foster advanced critical thinking.

Contextualized Real-World Example (Tabaco Albay)

Problem: A passenger ferry departs from Tabaco City Port toward San Miguel Island along a straight bearing north. Simultaneously, a fishing boat leaves the port heading east. After 1 hour, the ferry is 12 km north and the fishing boat is 5 km east. What is the direct distance between the two vessels?

Solution (Pythagorean Theorem):
Distance² = 12² + 5² = 144 + 25 = 169.
Distance = √169 = 13 kilometers.
